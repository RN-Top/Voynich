# Outside writing: every place where ordinary script appears (inventory, October 2026)

These are the only places where the manuscript shows writing in a known alphabet. They are the only possible
direct "ground" between Voynich words and meaning. Sources: ZL3b page notes and transcription. This is a list,
not a test.

| Where | What | Can it ground a Voynich word? |
|---|---|---|
| f1r, right margin | A column of Roman letters (a b c d e f g h i k … o p q r), added after the main text, badly aligned | No Voynich letter is written beside each Roman one, so it is not a key. The anomaly scan found it lacks the commonest Voynich letters. |
| f1r, bottom | Signature of Jacobus de Tepenecz (an owner, about 1600) | No: an ownership mark, much later. |
| f66r, bottom left | Four words "in a different hand and a different alphabet" beside four Voynich words, near a lying figure | Possibly, but the outside words are not transcribed in ZL3b. They need reading from the image. |
| f70v2 (Pisces) centre | The month name **"Mars"** in Roman letters, beside one Voynich word: **otolal** | **The best candidate pair in the book.** otolal occurs nowhere else, so nothing can confirm it. A close form, otalal, occurs 4 times (f58r, f70v2, f72v1, f107v). |
| f70v1 (Aries) and f71v (Taurus) centres | "Abril", "May" in Roman letters | No Voynich word is written beside them in the centre. |
| Other zodiac pages | Month names in Roman letters (ZL3b notes three explicitly) | The month names are generally thought to be a later addition by another hand, so they may not reflect the author's own words. |
| f116v (last page) | Mixed line: Voynich **oror sheey** followed by "valsch vbren so nim gaf mich o", plus "+ nuchicon oladab… + multo… + porta… + maria +" | The only place where Voynich words sit inside a real-language sentence. The surrounding words look like German ("so nim … mich") and Latin ("multos", "portas", "maria"). Who wrote it and when is disputed. |

## What this gives us

- **Two candidate grounds, both weak:** *otolal* beside "Mars" (f70v2), and *oror sheey* inside the German/Latin line on
  f116v. Each occurs once, so neither can be tested statistically.
- **What would help:** a reading of the four outside words on f66r from a high-resolution image, and comparison texts in
  medieval German and Latin to see whether f116v's language matches the grammar patterns found in the main text.
