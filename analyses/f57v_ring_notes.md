Not a hypothesis test: there is nothing to compare against by chance here. This file records what the ring is.

## Ring 3 (`f57v.3`)

- **Structure:** exactly **68 symbols = 17 × 4.** With a period of 17, only 3 of the 51 symbol comparisons
  differ. No other period from 10 to 24 comes close (all have 45 or more mismatches).
- **The 17-symbol unit:**

  `o l d r v x k m f @169v t r @170 @171 y c @172`

- **Variants between repeats:**
  - position 3 is `j` in the first repeat (`[d:j]`) and `d` otherwise;
  - position 16 is `I` in the first repeat and `c` otherwise;
  - position 9 is `f` in repeats 1–2 and `p` in repeats 3–4.

  So the four repeats come as two pairs, f-f / p-p. In the Voynich script, `f` and `p` are the same gallows letter
  with and without a loop.
- **Rare symbols:** `@170` and `@171` occur **only** in this ring. `@169` and `@172` occur here and once each in the
  single-letter column down the left margin of **f66r** (ZL3b itself comments "cf f57v" there).

## Medieval reading (interpretation, not tested)

- **A volvelle or alphabet wheel:** the ring repeats 4 times, once per quarter, like a dial graduated in four
  quadrants of 17 steps. 4 × 17 = 68.
- **17 is the key number of Jabir's "science of the balance"** (1 + 3 + 5 + 8 = 17, the proportions of the four
  qualities). This is a striking match with the alchemy discussion, but one number matching is easy by chance, so it
  is only a lead.
- **The rare symbols point outward:** the ring's rare symbols appear nowhere except the f66r margin column. That
  supports the idea that the ring is a key or index used elsewhere in the book.
