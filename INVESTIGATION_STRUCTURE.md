# Investigation Structure: Tracing the Origin

## The Central Mystery

A systematic medical framework (Condition → Cause → Cure) appears in:
1. **Rhazes' texts** (9th century, Baghdad)
2. **Fermoy manuscript** (1400, Ireland)
3. **Voynich manuscript** (1404-1438, unknown origin)

**Question: Did Rhazes originate it, or systematize something older?**

---

## Three Investigation Paths

### PATH 1: TRACING BACKWARD (Before Rhazes)

**What came BEFORE Rhazes?**

Investigation targets:
- Medical texts from 7th-8th centuries
- Pre-Islamic medical traditions (Greek, Persian, Indian)
- Earlier systematization attempts
- Rhazes' documented training and sources
- Earlier Baghdad medical institutions
- Galen, Hippocrates, and ancient medical frameworks
- Islamic medical development before Rhazes

**Key questions:**
1. Who trained Rhazes? (Historical records?)
2. What texts did he study?
3. Did older medical traditions use similar frameworks?
4. Was the Condition → Cause → Cure pattern already established?

**Evidence to find:**
- Rhazes' own references to his sources
- Pre-Rhazes medical texts showing similar organization
- Historical records of his training
- Earlier medical schools or institutions

---

### PATH 2: UNDERSTANDING RHAZES (The Central Figure)

**Who was Rhazes, really?**

Investigation targets:
- His biographical records
- His role as hospital administrator
- His teaching methods
- Why HIS systematization became standard (not others')
- His innovations vs. what he inherited
- His documented students and followers
- How his texts were used in medieval universities

**Key questions:**
1. Did Rhazes invent the framework or formalize existing practice?
2. What made his systematization different/better than others?
3. Why did his texts become THE standard curriculum?
4. How did he structure knowledge for teaching?

**Evidence to find:**
- His writings about methodology
- Historical records of his students
- How universities adopted his texts
- Comparisons to other physicians' approaches

---

### PATH 3: TRACING FORWARD (After Rhazes)

**How did Rhazes' work spread and transform?**

Investigation targets:
- Latin translations (12th century+)
- Universities that adopted his texts
- Adaptations in different regions (Irish, Spanish, German, etc.)
- How his framework appears in Fermoy
- How his framework appears in Voynich
- Other medieval manuscripts showing the same framework
- The teaching networks that spread his work

**Key questions:**
1. Which universities adopted Rhazes first?
2. How did his texts reach Ireland?
3. How did the framework get adapted to different languages?
4. Why does Voynich show the same organization?
5. Are there OTHER texts with this framework we haven't found?

**Evidence to find:**
- Translation history of Rhazes' works
- University curricula records
- Manuscript genealogies (which copies led to which)
- Connection to Irish medical training
- Connection to Voynich author's potential background

---

## The Real Investigation

**NOT:** "Rhazes is the origin"

**BUT:** 

1. **TRACE BACK:** What were Rhazes' sources? Did he originate or systematize?
2. **UNDERSTAND:** Why did his approach become standard? What made it teachable?
3. **TRACE FORWARD:** How did his systematization spread? Why does it appear in Fermoy and Voynich?

**The answer might be:**
- Rhazes originated the framework (unlikely but possible)
- Rhazes systematized an older tradition (likely)
- Rhazes was part of a gradual evolution of medical thinking (very likely)
- The framework wasn't unique to Rhazes but his VERSION spread (quite possible)

**We don't know yet. That's what we're investigating.**

---

## Evidence Hierarchy

### Tier 1: Primary Sources (Highest Value)
- Rhazes' own writings and references to his sources
- Contemporary records of his training
- Early medical texts he would have known
- University records of text adoption

### Tier 2: Secondary Evidence (High Value)
- Medical historian analyses of pre-Rhazes tradition
- Manuscript genealogies (which copies came from which)
- Translation history records
- Contemporary physician records

### Tier 3: Comparative Evidence (Supporting)
- Similar frameworks in other medical traditions
- Evidence from Fermoy about its sources
- Evidence from Voynich about author's training
- Other medieval manuscripts with same framework
