## Step 1: RHAZES (9th century, Baghdad)

**Person:** Al-Razi (865-925 CE), Persian physician at Baghdad medical school

**Primary Works:**
- Al-Hawi fi al-Tibb (Liber Continens) - Medical encyclopedia
- Al-Mansuri (Liber Almansoris) - 10-book medical treatise
- De Variolis et Morbillis - Systematic disease descriptions

**Framework:** **Condition → Cause → Cure** (systematic pedagogical structure)

**Status:** Definitive medical texts used across medieval Europe for 1000+ years

**Evidence Type:** Original Arabic texts, Latin translations, surviving manuscripts in 50+ European libraries

---

## Step 2: GERARD OF CREMONA (12th century, Toledo)

**Person:** Gerard of Cremona (1114-1187), Latin translator

**Work:** Translated Rhazes' medical texts from Arabic to Latin

**Primary Result:** Liber Almansoris (Latin translation of Rhazes' Al-Mansuri)

**Archive Locations:**
- Cambridge University Library MS Add. 9213
- Bodleian Library MS Canon. Misc. 412

**Significance:** Made Rhazes accessible to European universities

**Status:** Gerard translated 87 works total; Rhazes was major medical component

## Step 4: TADHG Ó CUINN (c.1400, Montpellier-trained Irish physician)

**Person:** Tadhg Ó Cuinn (fl. 1415), first documented Irish physician with Continental university degree

**Training:** Montpellier Medical School

**Primary Source Document:**

| Attribute | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| **Manuscript** | Trinity College Dublin MS 1343 (H.3.22) |
| **Title** | Materia Medica (Irish) |
| **Date** | Completed October 18, 1415 (colophon dated) |
| **Colophon Text** | "Compiled according to the antidotaries and herbals of the city of Salerno, according to the consensus of the college of the doctors of **Montpellier**" |
| **Degree** | "*Baistillerach a fisiceacht*" (Bachelor of Physic) |
| **Pages** | 291 alphabetically-arranged medical entries |
| **Surviving Copies** | 19 documented manuscript copies across Irish archives |
| **Archive** | Trinity College Dublin, National Library of Ireland, Royal Irish Academy |
| **Digital Access** | https://celt.ucc.ie/published/G600005.html |

**Critical Evidence:** His own colophon explicitly states his training was from "the college of the doctors of Montpellier" — the SAME MONTPELLIER SCHOOL that Gerard de Solo taught at.

**Significance:** 
- FIRST documented Irish physician with formal Continental university training
- Directly connected to Montpellier's Rhazes-based curriculum
- Returned to Ireland and began translating Continental texts to Irish
