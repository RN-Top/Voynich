## The Search Strategy (Phase 2 Roadmap)

### STEP 1: Identify Target Texts
**What to search for**: Latin medical texts, 1200-1350
**Where they are**: British Library, Vatican, Prague, Kraków, Hungarian archives
**Why these**: Widely copied, taught, and adapted = high probability of being source

**Key candidates**:
- Regimen Sanitatis
- Circa Instans (Urso of Salerno)
- Antidotarium Nicolai
- Liber Urinarum
- Prague/Kraków medical texts
- Hungarian medical traditions

### STEP 2: Analyze Each Text
**Tool**: `python3 analyses/source_text_analyzer.py`
**What it detects**:
- Language (is it Latin original?)
- Framework strength (Symptoms→Causes→Cures pattern)
- Teaching indicators (repetition, measurements, timing)
- Dating clues (Galen? Avicenna? Modern references?)
- Likelihood of being ORIGINAL SOURCE

**Success criteria**:
```
✓ Language: Latin (universal teaching language)
✓ Framework: STRONG (clear, consistent Symptoms→Causes→Cures)
✓ Dating: ~1200-1300 (oldest = closest to original)
✓ Teaching: Clear indicators (formulaic, repetitive, pedagogical)
✓ Adaptations: Evidence of Irish, Voynich, other versions
✓ History: Connection to formal teaching institution
```

### STEP 3: Comparative Analysis
**Compare each text against**:
- Fermoy (Irish adaptation)
- Voynich (cipher adaptation)
- Other known adaptations

**Look for**:
- Matching organizational patterns
- Same framework application
- Evidence of adaptation from source

### STEP 4: Historical Verification
**Documentary proof needed**:
- Was this text used in Eastern European schools?
- Did Irish physicians study there?
- Is there evidence of translation/adaptation?
- Can we trace your ancestor's training?
