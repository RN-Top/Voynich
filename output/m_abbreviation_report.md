# Is line-final -m/-am a space-saving device?

Pre-registration: `analyses/m_abbreviation_prereg.md` (committed before this code). 10,000 permutations, seed 20261001.

**Decision (pre-registered rule): H_space supported**

| Prediction | Observed | p | H_space predicts |
|---|---|---:|---|
| P1 space pressure: m-final lines vs other lines, glyphs above folio median | +0.00 vs -0.12 (diff +0.12) | 0.38 | diff > 0 |
| P2 -m/-am rate at end of paragraph-final lines vs other lines | 8.5% vs 16.1% (lower at paragraph ends) | 0.0001 | lower |
| P3 length of line-final form minus its stem's mid-line forms | -m/-am -0.36 vs other -0.08 glyphs | 0.0001 | -m/-am more negative |
| P4 share of line-final tokens whose stem also occurs mid-line | -m/-am 89.8% vs other 83.3% | 0.0002 | not lower (not decisive) |

Counts: P1 3,375 non-final lines (543 m-final); P2 729 paragraph-final
lines vs 3,375 others; P3 3,458 line-final tokens with a stem seen mid-line (543 -m/-am);
P4 4,104 line-final tokens (605 -m/-am).

## Reading the result (added after the first run; no numbers changed)

- **P2 carries most of the weight.** -m/-am is markedly rarer at the end of paragraphs, where the scribe
  was not short of space. A unit-terminator reading ("seal", "finis", "flush") predicts the opposite.
- **P3 is partly built in.** The length difference mostly reflects ending length: -am/-m are short endings.
  It cannot separate "abbreviated form" from "a short ending that prefers line ends".
- **P1 failed.** Lines ending in -m/-am are not measurably fuller, so this is not simply "ran out of room".
- **P4:** -m/-am words reuse ordinary stems. They are normal words in a variant form, not a special vocabulary.

Best current reading: -m/-am is a line-end variant form, used where a line breaks inside running text
and avoided where a paragraph ends. That fits a scribal line-end convention better than an end-of-unit marker.
