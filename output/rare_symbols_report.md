## Reading the result

- **Rare symbols land in first lines about 4× more than chance**: page-first lines 32 vs 8.3, paragraph-first
  lines 36 vs 9.6 (both p = 0.0001). The top lines of pages and paragraphs really are written differently.
- **The two most common rare symbols, @132 and @133, sit inside the first word of a line** (16 of 21 times),
  mostly on pharmacy pages. They behave like decorated or special forms used at the start of a unit, the way
  medieval scribes decorated initial letters. That is one likely explanation of the whole effect, not proven.
- **@175 is the opposite**: it ends words at the end of lines (6 of 7), a line-end form like -m/-am.
- **@169–@172** occur only in the f57v ring and the f66r labels, the "key-like" sequences found earlier.
- **f56r's two rare symbols lead elsewhere:**
  - **@167** appears on f56r (first word) and in the **star labels on f68r1 and f68r2**, each time as a first word.
  - **@168** appears on f56r and on the pharmacy page **f100r**.
  
  So this plant page shares special symbols with a star page and a pharmacy page. One shared symbol is too little
  to prove a link, but these are concrete pages to compare by eye.
