# Research Sources: Finding the Origin of the Teaching School

## Phase 1: Medieval Medical Texts to Search

We're looking for a **Latin source text** from ~1200-1300 that shows:
- ✓ Clear Symptoms→Causes→Cures framework
- ✓ Evidence of formal teaching institution
- ✓ Multiple regional adaptations derived from it
- ✓ Eastern European medical school connection (per hypothesis)

### Priority 1: Most Likely Sources

These texts are known to have been widely copied, taught, and adapted:

#### 1. **Regimen Sanitatis** (12th-13th century)
- **Original Latin text** - teaches health and medical knowledge
- **Framework pattern**: Conditions → Causes → Treatments
- **Spread**: Copied throughout Europe, adapted to regional languages
- **Status**: Multiple versions exist in British Library, Vatican
- **Why it matters**: Foundational medical teaching text widely adapted
- **To check**: BL MS Sloane 282, BL MS Egerton 2572

#### 2. **Circa Instans** (Urso of Salerno, 12th century)
- **Original Latin herbal** - systematically teaches plant remedies
- **Framework pattern**: Herb description → Properties → Uses
- **Spread**: Widely copied, especially in Eastern European schools
- **Status**: Vatican Library, Salerno medical archive
- **Why it matters**: Key Eastern Mediterranean medical tradition
- **To check**: Vatican MS Pal. lat. 1198, Biblioteca Medica Laurenziana

#### 3. **Antidotarium Nicolai** (13th century)
- **Original Latin text** - teaches preparation of medicines
- **Framework pattern**: Symptom/condition → Preparation → Application
- **Spread**: Standard medical school text (Prague, Bologna, others)
- **Status**: Multiple manuscripts in European libraries
- **Why it matters**: Explicitly a teaching text, formal structure
- **To check**: Czech National Library, Kraków University Archive

#### 4. **Liber Urinarum** (Isaac Israeli, translated 12th century)
- **Original Hebrew/Latin** - teaching text on diagnosis from urine
- **Framework pattern**: Symptom → Cause → Diagnosis
- **Spread**: Translated into Irish, Hebrew, regional languages
- **Status**: Royal Irish Academy Library (relevant!)
- **Why it matters**: Connected to Irish medical tradition
- **To check**: RIA MS D II 3, MS 23 E 29 (FERMOY itself!)

### Priority 2: Eastern European Medical Texts

These schools likely taught the framework (per hypothesis):

#### 5. **Prague University Medical Works** (1340+)
- Early formal medical university (1348)
- Likely adapted teaching framework from earlier sources
- **Archive**: Czech National Library, Charles University Archive
- **Status**: Need to search catalog: https://www.nkp.cz/

#### 6. **Kraków University Medical Texts** (1400s)
- Medical faculty established 1364
- Significant Eastern European medical tradition
- **Archive**: Jagiellonian University Library
- **Status**: Digitized collection (some online)

#### 7. **Hungarian Medical Tradition** (1300-1400)
- Royal court physicians
- Translated texts for medical training
- **Archive**: National Library of Hungary, Budapest
- **Status**: Manuscript catalogs available
